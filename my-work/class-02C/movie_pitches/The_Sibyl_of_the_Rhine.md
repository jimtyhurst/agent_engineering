**Box-Office Potential Report: *The Sibyl of the Rhine***

### 1. Executive Summary & Market Positioning
*The Sibyl of the Rhine* is a prestige historical biographical drama chronicling the life of Hildegard of Bingen—a 12th-century Benedictine nun, polymath, composer, and mystic. Positioned firmly within the upscale specialty film market, this project appeals to audiences drawn to intelligent, visually arresting period dramas centering on extraordinary women (e.g., *Portrait of a Lady on Fire*, *Benedetta*, *Amadeus*, and *The Favourite*). 

While not designed as a four-quadrant summer blockbuster, the film possesses strong specialized commercial viability driven by its rich visual palette (luminous divine visions), thematic resonance with modern audiences (female autonomy, artistic expression, and institutional resistance), and crossover appeal in European art-house and North American specialty markets (A24, Focus Features, or Bleecker Street distribution profiles).

---

### 2. Comparable Film Performance Analysis

To evaluate the box-office potential of *The Sibyl of the Rhine*, we look at recent specialty historical and religious biographical dramas:

*   ***Benedetta* (2021) | Dir. Paul Verhoeven**
    *   *Premise:* A 17th-century nun in Italy experiences erotic and religious visions, sparking controversy and political fallout.
    *   *Box Office Performance:* Grossed roughly $2.7M worldwide on a modest European art-house budget. While provocative and tonally distinct from our project, it proved that historical nun-centric dramas with mystical and political elements can capture significant cultural conversation, festival buzz (Cannes), and specialty theatrical traction.

    *   *Premise:* A visually stunning 18th-century period drama about female artistry, autonomy, and quiet rebellion.
    *   *Box Office Performance:* Grossed $7.5M worldwide. It demonstrated that exquisite period aesthetics, intellectual depth, and strong female-led narratives can achieve breakout success in the global art-house circuit and maintain high ancillary value (streaming, physical media).
*   ***Amadeus* (1984 / Theatrical re-releases & Classical Legacy) / *Tár* (2022)**
    *   *Premise:* Deep dives into genius, creative obsession, and institutional politics. (*Tár* grossed ~$6M worldwide as a prestige adult drama).
    *   *Takeaway:* Musically driven prestige dramas that emphasize creative process and visionary genius hold immense appeal for older, high-income art-house demographics, festival circuits, and awards voters.

### **Casting Proposals**

#### **1. Hildegard of Bingen (Adult / Magistra)**
* **Suggested Actor:** **Vicky Krieps** *(Phantom Thread, Corsage, The Dead Don't Hurt)*
* **Why the fit:** Vicky Krieps possesses a rare screen presence—simultaneously fierce, intellectual, and deeply vulnerable. In films like *Corsage*, she excelled at portraying a brilliant, independent woman chafing against the rigid, patriarchal constraints of her era. Krieps has the dramatic range to capture Hildegard’s internal torment with her blinding visions, her fierce artistic and intellectual brilliance, and her unyielding political resolve when standing up to male church authorities.

#### **2. Jutta of Sponheim (Hildegard’s Mentor & First Abbess)**
* **Suggested Actor:** **Harriet Walter** *(Succession, Ted Lasso, The Last Duel)*
* **Why the fit:** Harriet Walter is a master of playing formidable, wise, and grounded authority figures with profound emotional depth. As Jutta—the noblewoman-turned-anchorite who shelters young Hildegard and shapes her early life—Walter would bring immense gravitas and a tender, protective maternal dynamic that anchors the emotional stakes of Act I.

#### **3. Bernard of Clairvaux (The Influential Church Leader)**
* **Suggested Actor:** **Mark Strong** *(Tár, 1917, The Imitation Game)*
* **Why the fit:** Bernard of Clairvaux was one of the most powerful and zealous ecclesiastical figures of 12th-century Europe. Mark Strong excels at playing formidable, intellectually intimidating men of high status who command every scene they enter. He can project both orthodox piety and the pragmatic political calculation required when Bernard evaluates Hildegard's visions for the Pope.

#### **4. Pope Eugenius III (The Pontiff Who Authenticates the Visions)**
* **Suggested Actor:** **Jonathan Pryce** *(The Two Popes, Game of Thrones, Wolf Hall)*
* **Why the fit:** Having famously played a pope, Jonathan Pryce brings instant historical weight, warmth, and diplomatic shrewdness to the role of Pope Eugenius III. He can portray a leader caught between the traditional patriarchy of the church and the undeniable, awe-inspiring spiritual force of Hildegard’s testimonies at the Synod of Trier.

#### **5. Young Hildegard (Child / Oblate in Act I)**
* **Suggested Actor:** **Lola Petticrew** *(Boiling Point, North Sea Connection)* or a rising young European talent.
* **Why the fit:** Needs to portray a sensitive, observant child carrying the terrifying weight of secret, luminous visions while isolated in the monastery at Disibodenberg, setting up the emotional core of the film's opening act.
